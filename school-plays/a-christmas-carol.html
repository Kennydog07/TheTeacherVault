A Christmas Carol

Dickens's ghost story for Christmas, with real Dickens quotations and a GCSE head start.

Dickens called it a "Ghost Story of Christmas", and this adaptation keeps the chills, the warmth and the best lines while making them quick and stageable for Years 5 and 6. Six Narrators carry the story, often in Dickens's own sentences, so the audience hears the music of his language. A Christmas Carol is one of the most widely studied GCSE English Literature set texts, so performing it in Year 6 gives children a real head start.

Synopsis

Prologue – Marley Was Dead

On a foggy London Christmas Eve, the Carollers sing God Rest Ye Merry, Gentlemen. The Narrators tell us that Jacob Marley has been dead for seven years, and introduce his partner, the miser Ebenezer Scrooge.

Scene 1 (Stave One) – The Counting-House

Bob Cratchit shivers by a fire of one coal. Scrooge's cheerful nephew Fred invites him to Christmas dinner and gets "Bah! Humbug!" in reply. Two gentlemen collecting for the poor are told to try the prisons and the workhouses.

Scene 2 (Stave One) – Marley's Ghost

Scrooge sees Marley's face in his door knocker. Then Marley's Ghost appears, wrapped in the chain he "forged in life", and warns that Three Spirits will come.

Scene 3 (Stave Two) – The Ghost of Christmas Past

Scrooge sees his lonely boyhood, his sister Fan, Mr Fezziwig's joyful ball, and Belle, who leaves him because "another idol", money, has replaced her.

Scene 4 (Stave Three) – The Ghost of Christmas Present

The Cratchits make the most of a small goose and a proud pudding, and Tiny Tim says "God bless us every one!" At Fred's party the guests play a game about "Uncle Scrooge", and the Spirit reveals Ignorance and Want.

Scene 5 (Stave Four) – The Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come

A silent, hooded Phantom shows Scrooge a "very cheap funeral", the Cratchits grieving beside an empty chair, and a neglected grave with his own name on it. "I am not the man I was."

Scene 6 (Stave Five) – Christmas Morning

Scrooge wakes laughing. He sends the prize turkey to the Cratchits, joins Fred for dinner and raises Bob's salary, and to Tiny Tim, who did NOT die, he becomes a second father. The bells ring out for the finale.

Prologue and 6 scenes · follows Dickens's five staves. A faithful, accessible adaptation of Charles Dickens's 1843 novella, written in British English. It follows Dickens's five staves in order, and over 30 short quotations are Dickens's own words, shown in bold and placed in the stave where they appear in the novel. Song 1 is the traditional carol; the others are new words to well-known carol tunes.

Themes & Curriculum Links

Big theme: Redemption and the possibility of change; generosity versus greed; social responsibility ("Mankind was my business"); poverty and the welfare of children; family and loneliness; and the meaning of Christmas.

English

Reading books from our literary heritage; inference about characters' motives; figurative language ("solitary as an oyster"); performing play scripts. A real head start for GCSE English Literature.

PSHE & Citizenship

Community, charities and how our choices affect others; loneliness and regret; kindness, generosity, forgiveness and the idea that people can change.

History

Victorian Britain: poverty, child labour, workhouses and the Poor Law, and how Christmas traditions grew in the 1840s.

Music

Singing traditional carols and new words to traditional melodies, in unison and in parts, and learning about the history of the carol.

Characters

  • Ebenezer Scrooge – A cold, lonely miser who goes from "Bah! Humbug!" to "as light as a feather" in one night. The lead – can be shared between three performers by stave.
  • Bob Cratchit – Scrooge's kind, poorly paid clerk, who even raises a toast to Scrooge on Christmas Day.
  • Fred – Scrooge's warm, generous nephew, who refuses to give up on his uncle.
  • Jacob Marley's Ghost – Scrooge's dead partner, dragging the chain he forged in life.
  • The Three Spirits – The gentle Ghost of Christmas Past, the jolly giant Ghost of Christmas Present and the silent, pointing Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come.
  • The Cratchits – Mrs Cratchit, Martha, Belinda, Peter and Tiny Tim – a poor but loving family.
  • Young Ebenezer, Fan, the Fezziwigs, Dick & Belle – Scrooge's past: easily doubled, as they appear in one scene only.
  • Narrators 1–6, plus Gentlemen, Businessmen, Ignorance & Want, the Turkey Boy and Carollers – Storytellers, small roles and a chorus of any size.

Script Sample: The Opening Scene

Here's how the play begins, so you can get a feel for the length of the lines and the humour.

A London street on Christmas Eve, 1843. Fog. A lamp-post. The CAROLLERS stand in a group with lanterns.

NARRATOR 1Marley was dead: to begin with.

NARRATOR 2Old Marley was as dead as a door-nail.

NARRATOR 5Secret, and self-contained, and solitary as an oyster.

Scrooge's counting-house. Bitterly cold. FRED bursts in, rosy-cheeked and smiling.

FREDA merry Christmas, uncle! God save you!

SCROOGEBah! Humbug!

FREDChristmas a humbug, uncle! You don't mean that, I am sure?

SCROOGEI do. Merry Christmas! What right have you to be merry? What reason have you to be merry? You're poor enough.

FREDCome, then. What right have you to be dismal? You're rich enough.

No. Every song is set to a well-known traditional tune or a simple chant, so a confident adult voice, a piano or a guitar is all you need. Photocopiable lyric sheets are included.
