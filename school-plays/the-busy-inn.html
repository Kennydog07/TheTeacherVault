The Busy Inn

A traditional nativity, told by the innkeeper's family on the busiest night of the year.

All the familiar parts are here: Mary and Joseph, the Angel Gabriel, the innkeepers, shepherds and their sheep, three kings, a sky full of stars and the animals in the stable. It's told with warmth, respect and a sprinkle of gentle humour, through the eyes of the family who run the Busy Inn in Bethlehem.

Age rangeAges 4–7 · Reception – Year 2 (EYFS/KS1)

Synopsis

Scene 1 – A Visitor for Mary

In Nazareth, the Angel Gabriel visits Mary with wonderful news: she will have a baby boy, God's own Son, and she will call him Jesus. Mary trusts God and says yes. But our story is about to move somewhere much, much busier…

Scene 2 – The Busy Inn

In Bethlehem, the Town Crier announces that everyone must return to their family's home town to be counted. Eli, Hannah, Miriam and Sam rush about sweeping, baking and fluffing pillows as Guests pour in and demand more soup. By bedtime there is not a single bed left, and Sam proudly hangs up the 'No Room' sign.

Scene 3 – Knock, Knock!

Mary, Joseph and their tired Donkey arrive in Bethlehem. Two grumpy Innkeepers slam their doors. At the Busy Inn, Sam reads them the sign, but Miriam sees how weary Mary is and remembers the warm, quiet stable at the back. The whole family leads the way with lanterns.

Scene 4 – The Hillside

On the dark hills, the Shepherds are trying (and failing) not to wake their Sheep. Suddenly Gabriel and the Angels fill the sky with light and bring good news of great joy: a Saviour has been born in Bethlehem. Off go the Shepherds, and the Sheep insist on coming too.

Scene 5 – The Stable

In the stable behind the inn, Jesus is born and laid in the manger. The Animals whisper hello, Sam tiptoes in, and Eli helpfully brings… soup. The Shepherds arrive quietly to see the baby the angel told them about, with a little lamb to keep him cosy.

Scene 6 – The Special Star

A brand-new star shines over Bethlehem, and three Kings follow it all the way to the stable with gifts of gold, frankincense and myrrh. The Busy Inn family realise that the inn was full, but the stable had room for everyone, and on the busiest night of the year the most important guest of all had come.

6 scenes · 153 spoken lines, mostly 2–8 words. A fresh retelling of the traditional Nativity story, written in British English. The three new songs have original words set to traditional tunes, and the four carols use their traditional words.

Themes & Curriculum Links

Big theme: Even on the busiest night, there is always room for love. Kindness and welcome matter, and the Christmas story is about the very special baby, Jesus, whose birth Christians celebrate at Christmas.

Understanding the World / RE

Knowing the Christmas story as told in the Christian tradition and why Christians celebrate Christmas; talking about special times and celebrations; comparing life long ago with life today; recognising that people celebrate different festivals.

PSED

Kindness and welcome: Miriam notices someone who needs help and finds a way to help. Thinking about how it feels to be tired, turned away, welcomed or amazed, and working as a team like the Busy Inn family.

Communication & Language

Speaking clearly with expression, listening for cues and joining in with repeated refrains. New vocabulary: inn, innkeeper, census, stable, manger, shepherd, angel, frankincense, myrrh.

Expressive Arts & Design

Singing traditional carols and new songs from memory with actions; moving imaginatively as bustling innkeepers, sleepy shepherds and twinkling stars; making crowns, star headbands and a stable set.

KS1 links

RE (Christianity – the Christmas story; follow your locally agreed syllabus), PSHE (caring friendships, kindness), Music (using voices expressively), English (spoken language; sequencing a familiar story) and History (life long ago).

Characters

  • Mary & Joseph (leads) – Kind, gentle Mary and steady, protective Joseph. Each part can be shared by two children, one per half of the play.
  • Narrators 1–3 – Link the scenes; best read by adults, teaching assistants or confident Year 2 readers.
  • The Angel Gabriel & the Angels – God's messenger, whose first words are always "Don't be afraid", and a sky full of shining helpers.
  • Eli, Hannah, Miriam & Sam – The Busy Inn family. Eli's answer to every problem is soup, and little Sam has just learned to read the words 'No room'.
  • The Town Crier, Innkeepers 1 and 2 & the Guests – Loud announcements, slammed doors and guests who want more soup – lovely comic parts.
  • The Shepherds & the Sheep – Sleepy shepherds and woolly sheep who say "Baa!" at exactly the wrong moments. Perfect for the youngest or shyest children.
  • Donkey & the Stable Animals – The Donkey has very tired feet; the Ox and friends are the first to whisper hello to the baby.
  • The Special Star, the Stars & the Three Kings – Melchior, Caspar and Balthazar follow the Special Star with their gifts.

Script Sample: The Opening Scene

Here's how the play begins, so you can get a feel for the length of the lines and the humour.

The stage is dark and quiet. The Stars sit at the back. Mary is at home in Nazareth, sweeping the floor. The Narrators stand to one side.

NARRATOR 1Long, long ago, in a little town called Nazareth, there lived a young woman called Mary.

NARRATOR 2Mary was kind and gentle, and she was going to marry a carpenter called Joseph.

Joseph walks past carrying a small wooden stool. He waves.

JOSEPHGood morning, Mary!

MARYGood morning, Joseph!

NARRATOR 3One ordinary day, something extraordinary happened.

A gentle chime. Gabriel enters with arms open wide, followed by the Angels.

GABRIELHello, Mary.

MARY(stepping back, surprised) Oh! Who are you?

GABRIELDon't be afraid. I am Gabriel. God has sent me with good news.

ALL ANGELSGood news!

Common Questions

How many children can take part?

The cast table shows exactly how to cast the play for 20, 25, 30 or 40 children, which roles can be doubled, and how many lines each part has. Angels, Guests, Shepherds, Sheep, Stars and Animals are group roles that can be as large or small as you need, there are tips for fewer than 20 or more than 40 children, and every child can be on stage for the finale.

Do we need sheet music or backing tracks?

No. Every song is set to a well-known traditional tune or a simple chant, so a confident adult voice, a piano or a guitar is all you need. Photocopiable lyric sheets are included.

What format is the pack?

A print-ready PDF (41 pages), delivered instantly by Gumroad after purchase. Print as many copies as your school needs.

Is it a traditional, religious nativity?

Yes. The Busy Inn tells the Christmas story as it is told in the Christian tradition, with Mary and Joseph, the angels, shepherds, kings and the birth of Jesus, plus four traditional carols. If you would prefer a non-religious winter play, see The Moon Who Was Scared of the Dark.
