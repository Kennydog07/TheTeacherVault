Lost Property

When the lights go out, the lost property box comes alive... and everything in it is trying to get home.

A comedy (with a fly called Kevin, a recorder that only knows one note and a chorus of nameless jumpers) that is also a play about belonging. Audiences laugh a lot and, usually, reach for a tissue near the end.

Synopsis

Scene 1 – Monday: The Notice

Mr Crumble the caretaker pins up a notice: everything not collected by Friday will be cleared out. When the lights go off, the items wake up and Pencil Case calls the Lost Property Society to order.

Scene 2 – Tuesday: Operation Trainer

Lefty the Single Trainer tries to find his brother (and ends up head-first in a mop bucket). Then Scarf tells her story: she was knitted by Nana Pearl, with a wish in every stitch.

Scene 3 – Wednesday: The Bear in the Book Corner

The items find Teddy, smuggled into school by Poppy, a new girl who was scared in her first week. No one gets left behind.

Scene 4 – Thursday: The Great Parade

With one night left, the items march to the hall to line up on the stage, freezing every time Mr Crumble's torch sweeps past.

Scenes 5–6 – Friday: The Clear-Out

Mr Crumble picks up Teddy and remembers being new at school himself. In assembly every item is reunited with its owner, and the cast sing "Where We Belong".

Themes & Curriculum Links

Big theme: Belonging: the people and things that make us feel at home, and the kindness that helps someone who feels lost. The leavers' version adds change and transition, ideal for the move to secondary school.

PSHE & Relationships Education

Families come in different forms; caring friendships; helping someone who feels lonely or left out; strategies for managing change, such as starting a new school or moving to secondary school.

English: spoken language & drama

Performances, role play, improvisation and debate (Years 3–6); preparing play scripts to read aloud and perform; character.

Year 6 transition

The alternative leavers' Scene 6 and final verse, with space to add children's own memories.

Characters

  • Pencil Case – Calls the Lost Property Society to order and drives the plot.
  • Lefty (Single Trainer) – A big comic role with hopping and slapstick, looking for his brother Righty.
  • Scarf – Knitted by Nana Pearl; a heartfelt monologue and solo song.
  • Lunchbox – Comedy then a moving speech (and a fly called Kevin).
  • Teddy (Mr Buttons) – Smuggled into school by Poppy; a gentle solo song.
  • Small item parts – Water Bottle, Glove, Recorder, Goggles, PE Shorts, two Odd Socks and the Jumpers.
  • The grown-ups – Mr Crumble the caretaker and Mrs Adebayo the headteacher.
  • The owners & groups – Arlo, Jaz, Kofi, Poppy, Pupils 1–4, Narrators 1–4, more Jumpers and the Children.

There are 29 named roles, many with small parts, plus flexible groups (Jumpers, Odd Socks, Children) that can include as many performers as you like, so it works for 20 to 32+ children. Younger casts can share longer speeches.

Script Sample: The Opening Scene

Here's how the play begins, so you can get a feel for the length of the lines and the humour.

A school corridor after home time. Centre stage: a big cardboard box labelled LOST PROPERTY, overflowing. The ITEMS are frozen in and around it.

MR CRUMBLE enters pushing a mop and bucket, whistling. He stops by the box and sighs.

MR CRUMBLELook at you. Overflowing again. Jumpers. Gloves. A recorder. (He picks up RECORDER, sniffs it and recoils.) Ugh. Somebody's been eating cheese and onion.

He takes a notice from his pocket and pins it up, reading it aloud.

MR CRUMBLE"NOTICE. All lost property not collected by FRIDAY will be cleared out. Signed, Mr Crumble, Caretaker." (He pats the box.) Sorry, old friends. Rules is rules.

He switches off the light. SFX: CLICK. He exits. Moonlight. A clock ticks.

NARRATOR 3Jumpers with no names. Gloves with no partners. Lunchboxes with... surprises inside.

Common Questions

How many children can take part?

There are 29 named roles, many with small parts, plus flexible groups (Jumpers, Odd Socks, Children) that can include as many performers as you like, so it works for 20 to 32+ children. Younger casts can share longer speeches.

Do we need sheet music or backing tracks?

No. Every song is set to a well-known traditional tune or a simple chant, so a confident adult voice, a piano or a guitar is all you need. Photocopiable lyric sheets are included.

What format is the pack?

A print-ready PDF (41 pages), delivered instantly by Gumroad after purchase. Print as many copies as your school needs.

Does it work as a Year 6 leavers' play?

Yes. An alternative Scene 6 and a new final verse are included, in which the items realise their owners are leaving for good and find a way to say goodbye. There is space to add the children's own memories.
