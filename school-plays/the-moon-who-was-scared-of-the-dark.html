The Moon Who Was Scared of the Dark

A gentle winter play about facing fears with friends.

Synopsis

Scene 1 – Night Falls

On the longest night of the winter, the tired Sun says goodnight. The stars and night animals wait for the Moon… but the Moon is hiding behind the Clouds. The Moon is scared of the dark! Little Mouse can't find the path home, and the animals promise to help.

Scenes 2–3 – Owl and Fox

Wise (and slightly show-off) Owl teaches the Moon to take a big brave breath. Fox claims to be scared of nothing, until a Hedgehog says hello, then shows the Moon how to stand up tall.

Scenes 4–5 – Fireflies and Hedgehogs

The blinking Fireflies tell the Moon that everyone has a little light inside. The shy Hedgehogs curl up small, count to three and then PEEP out.

Scene 6 – The Sleepy Star

A very Sleepy Star reminds the Moon that nobody is ever alone in the sky. When Little Mouse says, "I really need you, Moon," the Moon takes the Star's hand.

Scene 7 – The Moon Glows

Using every bravery idea, the Moon rises high and glows. Little Mouse finds the way home, the Snowflakes dance, and everyone sings a gentle goodnight song.

Themes & Curriculum Links

Big theme: Everybody feels scared sometimes. With a few brave ideas and good friends beside us, we can face our fears – and help others too.

PSED

Talking about fears and worries; simple calming strategies (deep breaths, standing tall, counting to three, holding a hand); helping others and accepting help.

Communication & Language

Speaking to an audience with expressive voices, joining in with repeated refrains, and new vocabulary (brave, glow, nocturnal, flicker, frosty, longest night).

Expressive Arts & Design

A cumulative song with actions, imaginative movement (fireflies, hedgehogs, clouds, snowflakes) and making masks and a 'glowing' moon.

Understanding the World & KS1

Day and night, seasons and nocturnal animals; KS1 Science (seasonal changes, animals), KS1 PSHE/RSE and KS1 Music.

Characters

  • The Moon (lead) – Scared of the dark! Can be shared by two or three children in matching costumes.
  • Narrators 1–3 – Best read by adults or confident readers.
  • The Sun – A small part in Scene 1 with a HUGE yawn.
  • Owl – Wise and slightly show-off: a good part for a confident speaker.
  • Fox – A lively comic part who is 'scared of nothing at all'.
  • Little Mouse – Small but important – the reason the Moon finally rises.
  • Fireflies & Hedgehogs – Group roles with a few speaking parts and group lines.
  • Sleepy Star – A wonderful comic part for a child who can do a big yawn!
  • Clouds, Stars & Snowflakes – Non-speaking movement and dance roles for anyone who is free.

The cast list shows how to cast for 15, 20, 25 or 30 children. With fewer than 15, use one adult Narrator and small groups; with more than 30, add extra Fireflies, Hedgehogs, Stars and Snowflakes, or night creatures such as Bats or Badgers.

Script Sample: The Opening Scene

Here's how the play begins, so you can get a feel for the length of the lines and the humour.

A frosty winter evening. The Sun stands high up at the back. The Clouds stand together holding up a cloud shape; the Moon is hiding behind it.

NARRATOR 1It was a cold, frosty winter evening. The longest night of the whole year.

NARRATOR 2Up in the sky, the Sun was very, very tired.

SUN(a HUGE yawn) Yaaaaawn! What a long day of shining. I'm off to bed!

NARRATOR 3The Sun stretched…

The Sun stretches. Everybody on stage stretches too.

NARRATOR 3…and yawned…

Everybody yawns. The Narrators invite the audience to yawn too.

SUNGoodnight, everybody! Moon – it's your turn!

The Sun waves and slowly sinks down behind the block, then tiptoes off.

NARRATOR 1And the sky went dark.

How many children can take part?

The cast list shows how to cast for 15, 20, 25 or 30 children. With fewer than 15, use one adult Narrator and small groups; with more than 30, add extra Fireflies, Hedgehogs, Stars and Snowflakes, or night creatures such as Bats or Badgers.

Do we need sheet music or backing tracks?

No. Every song is set to a well-known traditional tune or a simple chant, so a confident adult voice, a piano or a guitar is all you need. Photocopiable lyric sheets are included.
