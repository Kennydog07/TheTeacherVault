The Odd Sock

A funny, warm-hearted play about being different.

Written for the very youngest performers in school. Every line is short, every song is easy to learn, and there are lots of chances to move, sing and join in together, so nobody needs to feel nervous about being on their own.

Synopsis

Scene 1 – The Laundry Basket

It's Monday morning and Stripy Sock wakes at the bottom of a very full, very smelly laundry basket. Its other sock is missing! Jumper, Vest and the Pyjamas (who match perfectly and like to say so) send Stripy Sock off to ask the Washing Machine.

Scene 2 – The Washing Machine

The robotic Washing Machine beeps Stripy Sock inside, where a crowd of cheerful Bubbles pop up to help. A dizzy spin cycle later, the Washing Machine suggests asking the Pegs.

Scene 3 – The Washing Line

The Pegs pinch Stripy Sock onto the line to look for its partner. When the Wind whooshes in, Stripy Sock goes flying over the fence, back through the window… and lands right next to the Tumble Dryer. Uh-oh.

Scene 4 – The Tumble Dryer

The grumpy Tumble Dryer is fed up of being blamed for every missing sock. But when Stripy Sock starts to cry, he softens and tells it where all the odd socks go: the Sock Drawer.

Scene 5 – The Sock Drawer

Inside the drawer is a gang of odd socks. None of them match, and that's the best bit! Stripy Sock realises you don't have to match to be friends, and everyone joins in a joyful finale.

Themes & Curriculum Links

Big theme: It's OK to be different. You don't have to match to be friends – our differences are what make us special.

PSED

Recognising and talking about feelings; building friendships and including others who seem different; understanding that it is OK to be different.

Communication & Language

Speaking clearly to an audience, listening for cues, new vocabulary (pair, match, odd, cycle, peg, lonely, different) and retelling a story in sequence.

Expressive Arts & Design

Singing songs from memory with actions, moving imaginatively, making simple costumes and props, and exploring pattern (stripes and spots).

KS1 links

KS1 PSHE/RSE (caring friendships and respectful relationships), KS1 Music (using voices expressively) and KS1 English (spoken language).

Characters

  • Stripy Sock (lead) – A cheerful red-and-blue sock looking for its partner. Can be shared by up to five children as a 'relay lead'.
  • Narrators 1–3 – Carry the story; best read by adults or confident readers.
  • Jumper, Vest & the Pyjamas – The laundry basket clothes. The two Pyjamas always speak together.
  • Washing Machine – A robot-like machine who speaks… one… word… at… a… time.
  • The Bubbles & the Pegs – Group roles with a few short lines each, plus group lines and actions.
  • The Wind & the Fluff – Movement and group-line roles, great for children who prefer not to speak alone.
  • Tumble Dryer – A brilliant comic part for a child who enjoys being grumpy!
  • The Odd Socks – Spotty, Fluffy, Holey, Sparkly, Football and Tiny, plus a non-speaking cameo for the Other Sock.

Script Sample: The Opening Scene

Here's how the play begins, so you can get a feel for the length of the lines and the humour.

The stage is a giant laundry basket. The Clothes (Jumper, Vest and the two Pyjamas) are curled up asleep inside. Stripy Sock is curled up in the middle.

NARRATOR 1Once upon a Monday morning, in a house not very far from here…

NARRATOR 2…there was a laundry basket. A very FULL laundry basket.

NARRATOR 3A very full, very SMELLY laundry basket.

All three Narrators hold their noses. The Clothes pop up one at a time, wave to the audience and flop back down.

NARRATOR 1And right at the bottom of the basket lived a sock. A stripy sock.

STRIPY SOCK(stretching and yawning) Good morning, everybody!

JUMPER(sleepily) Morning, Stripy Sock.

VESTMorning!

PYJAMAS(together, doing the same stretch) Mor-ning!

STRIPY SOCKGood morning, my other sock! (pause) My other sock?

NARRATOR 2Stripy Sock looked left.

Stripy Sock looks left. The Clothes look left too.
